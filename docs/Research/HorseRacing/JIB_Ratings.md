# JIB Ratings — Key Insights and Interesting Facts  
*Summary extracted from the discussion on The UK Betting Forum*  
*Source: JIB Ratings thread*

---

## 1. Market‑Defined Class  
JIB determines race class using the **average Official Ratings of the first 5 forecast horses** (non‑handicaps) or **first 6** (handicaps).  
This means class is defined by **market expectation**, not race results — an inversion of traditional handicapping logic.

---

## 2. Aggressive Beaten‑Distance Formula  
JIB uses:



\[
\text{lbs} = \frac{\text{lengths beaten} \cdot 15}{\text{race distance (f)}} 
\]



Implications:

- 2L in **5f** → **6 lbs**  
- 2L in **7f** → **≈4 lbs**  
- 2L in **12f** → **≈2.5 lbs**

One of the steepest public beaten‑distance scales.  
Some contributors even suggest **17.5** instead of **15**.

---

## 3. System Focus: Beatable Opposition  
JIB’s method is not about finding the “best” horse.  
It’s about identifying **when a horse meets the weakest opposition it has faced for a long time**.

Example: Fantasy Believer tracked across **16 races** until conditions aligned.

---

## 4. Divergence from Official Handicapper  
Fantasy Believer’s case shows:

- OH dropped the horse **10 lbs**  
- JIB’s ratings showed it running **to the level of the opposition**, not its finishing position

JIB reacts to **context**, not results.

---

## 5. Median OR > Average OR  
Median OR is argued to be a better class measure because average OR is distorted by:

- one very high‑rated horse  
- one very low‑rated horse  
- small fields  
- unusual market structures

Median stabilizes class estimation.

---

## 6. Hybrid Rating Approaches  
Forum members combine JIB with:

- **VDW ability ratings**  
- **Bowers balanced/unbalanced race logic**  
- **TBBHC (TheBluesBrother handicap column)**  
- **Timeform speed figures**

A rare multi‑system synthesis rather than “one true method”.

---

## 7. Toyotomi: Multi‑System Convergence  
Toyotomi was simultaneously:

- top‑rated by JIB  
- a VDW “form horse”  
- supported by Timeform speed figures  
- placed well by trainer patterns  
- confirmed by market compression (BSP 2.34)

A case where **ratings, form logic, speed, trainer angles, and market behaviour aligned**.

---

## 8. System Failure Clusters  
The OP mentions hitting **7 losers at Epsom**.  
This reflects a known phenomenon:

> When a system fails, it often fails across multiple races at the same track or meeting.

Environmental bias, not randomness.

---

## 9. Surface‑Specific Form  
Multiple examples show AW vs turf form behaving like **different species**:

- Toyotomi  
- Jungle Drums  
- Chalk Mountain  
- Dangerman  
- Spioradalta

Modern racing increasingly requires **surface‑segmented** analysis.

---

## 10. Consistency as a Meta‑Rating  
The OP uses a **consistency factor** as a final filter.  
This mirrors:

- VDW form logic  
- RPR consistency flags  
- Timeform symbols  
- Bowers’ balanced race logic

Consistency is becoming a universal cross‑system signal.
