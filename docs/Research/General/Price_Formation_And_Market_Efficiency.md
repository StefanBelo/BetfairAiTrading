# Price Formation and Market Efficiency
 on Betfair

## 1. The Mechanics of Price Formation
Unlike traditional bookmakers who set odds based on their own risk assessments and profit margins, Betfair is a peer-to-peer exchange. Prices are formed purely by the interaction of **Supply (Lay)** and **Demand (Back)**.

*   **Equilibrium:** The traded price at any given moment is the equilibrium point where the collective "opinion" of the market meets.
*   **Matched Volume Pressure:** Unlike pending offers (WOM), matched volume represents real capital committed to the market. High velocity and large trade sizes at a specific price point indicate aggressive "attacking" of that level.
*   **Weight of Money (WOM) vs. History:** It is important to distinguish between **Market Depth** (unmatched offers/intentions) and **Price History** (matched trades/reality). While WOM can be "spoofed" by bots, matched history provides an indisputable record of where value has been found.
*   **Correlated Movements (The See-Saw Effect):** In a closed market, the probabilities of all selections must sum to ~100%. Therefore, a strong move on the favorite (steam) often forces an inverse move (drift) on the 2nd or 3rd favorites. This correlation is a key lead indicator for technical traders.



## 2. The Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH)
In the context of betting exchanges, the EMH suggests that the market price reflects all currently available information.

*   **Pre-Race Inefficiency:** In the minutes or hours leading up to a race, the market can be inefficient. Small pieces of information (paddock looks, weather changes) or emotional betting can cause short-term price deviations.
*   **The "Off" Efficiency:** As the race start (the "off") approaches, the market becomes exponentially more efficient. The **Betfair Starting Price (BSP)** is statistically one of the most accurate predictors of outcome probability in existence.

## 3. Price vs. True Probability
It is vital to distinguish between what the market *thinks* will happen and what *will* actually happen.

*   **Wisdom of the Crowd:** Large groups of diverse participants (informed syndicates, casual bettors, and market-making bots) collectively estimate probability better than any single individual.
*   **Implicit Probability:** A price of 2.0 implies a 50% probability. If the market is efficient, a horse priced at 2.0 will win almost exactly 50% of the time over a large sample size.

## 4. Finding a Trading Edge
If the market were perfectly efficient at all times, profitable trading would be impossible. Traders exploit the **gaps** in efficiency:

1.  **Value (EV+):** Identifying when the market's "opinion" (the price) is significantly different from the true statistical probability of the outcome.
2.  **Predictive Momentum (Trend Swap):** Identifying when the market's consensus is *about* to change. A "Trend Swap" often occurs when a steamer hits a resistance level, and the market momentum pivots to a secondary favorite.
3.  **Mean Reversion (The Anchor):** Using the **Opening Price** as an anchor. If a price has drifted significantly due to temporary noise, it often has a statistical tendency to revert toward the early morning "pure opinion" price.
4.  **Matched Volume Nodes:** Identifying specific price points where the largest volumes of money have *already* changed hands. These nodes act as psychological barriers where prices are likely to "bounce" (support) or struggle to break through (resistance).



## Summary
The Betfair price is a **real-time consensus of value**, not a fixed probability. Successful trading requires understanding the psychology and data flow that causes this consensus to shift.
